What we do that works

We treat people as people. The rest gets measured.

People first

What usually goes wrong: the same program for everyone, the child treated as a grade level, the municipality treated as "a medium-sized municipality".

What we do: we start from the concrete person, by name, and design from there.

Brain and body

What usually goes wrong: talking about motivation and well-being without knowing how attention, sleep, memory or blood pressure actually work.

What we do: a physician and a neuroscience PhD in the room, not in a citation.

Classic ideas that lasted

What usually goes wrong: this year's trend with an English name.

What we do: Minsky, Papert and decades of evidence about how people learn, applied today.

Technology used well

What usually goes wrong: the platform nobody uses and the licence that gets paid anyway.

What we do: we build our own tools and only place them where they help the person.

Systems thinking

What usually goes wrong: solving one symptom and creating two.

What we do: we map what connects grades to sleep, isolation to health, training to the service that receives it, and act where the effect is biggest.

Testing

What usually goes wrong: the plan in a slide deck that was never tried out.

What we do: a small pilot, with real people, before any scale-up.

Monitoring results

What usually goes wrong: at year end, nobody knows what changed.

What we do: we define what gets measured before starting, and come back with numbers you can read out loud.

Adapting to people

What usually goes wrong: schedules, jargon and formats built for the supplier's convenience.

What we do: we go where people are, at the time they can, in the language they speak.

Where the name comes from

Minsky and Papert

McMinsky comes from Marvin Minsky. The pedagogy comes from Seymour Papert. They worked together at MIT and co-authored the book that dismantled a trend ("Perceptrons", 1969), which already says what this house thinks of trends.

Minsky
Society of mind

Intelligence does not come from a single principle, it comes from many simple pieces working together.

Application: we do not sell "the method". We assemble the right set of pieces for each person and each population.

Minsky
Learning more than one way

You only truly understand something once you have learned it through more than one path.

Application: every subject taught through two or three paths: symbol, image, body, simulation.

Papert
Constructionism

You learn by building something you can show to others.

Application: you leave with something made, never just slides.

Papert
Hard fun

Children do not want it to be easy, they want it to be hard and fun at the same time.

Application: that is "Deep work. Deep fun." Now you know where it comes from.
