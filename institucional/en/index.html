The problem as they live it

The program nobody measures

A school success program is bought almost always the same way: a psychologist on retainer, one year at a time, and at the end nobody can say what changed. It is not bad will, it is that nobody defined what would be measured, and what is not measured repeats itself next year under another name.

Ageing treated as entertainment

Recreational activities to fill a morning, with nobody looking at hearing, blood pressure or the isolation behind the next fall. Ageing is not entertainment, it is public health, and it should be treated as such.

Training that gets signed off and does not stick

One session, one photo for the report, and workers go back to the same software they were not using before. Training that does not leave something concrete behind does not change anyone's day-to-day.

Topics

School success and mental health in schools

Portugal is at the OECD average in mathematics, reading and science, and has been declining since 2018 (OECD, PISA 2022).

Ageing and isolation

Health literacy for seniors and caregivers, with a physician, and social programs with purpose.

Portugal had 24.3% of its population aged 65 or over in 2025, the second most aged country in the European Union (Eurostat).

Inclusion of immigrants and language

Portuguese as a host language, training of mediators and accessible content.

Among students of immigrant origin in Portugal, the socioeconomic profile is similar to the rest of the student population: language, not poverty, is the first barrier (OECD, PISA 2022).

Digital upskilling for citizens and workers

Training in digital tools for non-technical people, with a real task at the end, not an attendance certificate.

34.1% of Portuguese adults have low combined literacy and numeracy performance (OECD, PIAAC 2023).

Heritage, culture and tourism

Content, video and scientific or cultural programming, with the same rigour as our own podcast.

Civil protection and vulnerable people

Risk literacy for seniors, schools and caregivers, and studies of populations vulnerable to heat or isolation.

Why believe us

Method with evidence

We do not invent what works. We use decades of research on how people learn, crossed with what we see every afternoon with students, families and now also with institutions. Active recall, spacing, small-group tutoring, fast feedback: none of this is new, what is missing is someone who puts it to work with real people and comes back with numbers.

Step by step

How we work

1. Listen

The first meeting is to listen, not to present. The client knows what troubles them.

2. Diagnose

We measure the starting point before proposing anything.

3. Design

The program is designed for the specific institution, not copied from another proposal.

4. Execute on the ground

With the municipality's, school's or charity's team, not above it. One point of contact and one room are enough.

5. Measure

What gets measured is defined before starting, so there are no surprises at the end.

6. Deliver

A report you can read out loud in a meeting, not forty pages to file away.

We know how public funding works, including EU funds, and we fit each proposal to the real capacity and most urgent needs of each institution, without lowering the standard. We work in weeks, not years.
