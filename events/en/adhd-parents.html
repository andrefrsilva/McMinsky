How many practical, simple, actually-working tricks can you apply tomorrow morning? Not principles. Not concepts. Tricks to use on Monday.

The 3 biggest ADHD myths — and what neuroscience actually says

Myth

"My child pays attention when they want to — ADHD is just an excuse not to try."

What neuroscience says

The ADHD brain has difficulty regulating attention consistently, not with paying attention in general. The child focuses on video games for hours because the brain releases dopamine differently with immediate and unpredictable stimuli. It's not a choice — it's biology.

Myth

"If I medicate them straight away I'll lose my child's personality."

What neuroscience says

Medication is one of several tools — it's not mandatory nor the only answer. For some children it's transformative. For others, other approaches work better. What doesn't make sense is deciding without understanding the mechanism.

Myth

"It's screens, diet, school that caused it."

What neuroscience says

ADHD has 70–80% heritability — it's one of the neurological conditions with the highest genetic component that exists. Screens and stress can worsen symptoms, not cause the condition. Understanding this distinction completely changes how you approach the problem.
